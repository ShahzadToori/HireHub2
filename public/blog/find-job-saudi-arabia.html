How to Find a Job in Saudi Arabia in 2026

A step-by-step guide from understanding the market to signing your offer letter.

7 min read June 2025 JobOrbit Editorial
Back to Blog

Saudi Arabia is one of the most exciting job markets in the world right now. Fuelled by Vision 2030 — the Kingdom's ambitious economic diversification programme — thousands of new roles emerge every month across technology, healthcare, finance, tourism, and construction. Whether you are a Saudi national entering the workforce or an experienced expat professional considering relocation, knowing how to navigate the Saudi job market is the difference between a frustrating months-long search and a signed offer letter in six weeks.

1. Understand the Job Market Landscape

Saudi Arabia's economy is transitioning rapidly. Historically dominated by oil revenues and government employment, the private sector now actively courts skilled professionals. The sectors with the highest demand in 2026 include:

  • Technology and IT: Software engineers, cybersecurity experts, cloud architects, and data scientists are in critical shortage across Riyadh and Jeddah alike.
  • Healthcare: Doctors, nurses, pharmacists, and allied health professionals remain in constant demand as Saudi Arabia expands its hospital and clinic network.
  • Construction and Engineering: NEOM, The Line, Diriyah Gate, and dozens of Vision 2030 megaprojects require civil, mechanical, and structural engineers at every level.
  • Finance and Banking: Saudi Arabia's growing capital markets require analysts, compliance officers, risk managers, and wealth management professionals.
  • Hospitality and Tourism: With 100 million tourist arrivals targeted by 2030, hotels, entertainment venues, airlines, and tour operators are hiring aggressively.

2. Polish Your CV for the Saudi Market

Saudi employers — both local companies and multinationals — have specific expectations. Your CV should be concise (no more than two pages), professional, and tailored to each individual role. Include a professional photo in the top-right corner, a short punchy professional summary, and clearly quantified achievements. Numbers create credibility: "increased team revenue by 34%" will always outperform "improved team performance."

Crucially, many companies in Saudi Arabia now use Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS). This means your CV needs to mirror the language in the job description. If the posting says "project management" and your CV says "programme delivery," the ATS may not match you. Mirror their exact vocabulary.

3. Use the Right Job Search Channels

There is no single platform that dominates the Saudi job market. A successful search uses multiple channels simultaneously:

  • Local job boards like JobOrbit list roles from companies across the Kingdom, including positions that never appear on international platforms.
  • LinkedIn is heavily used by multinational companies and senior-level hiring in Saudi Arabia. A complete, keyword-rich profile is essential.
  • Recruitment agencies such as Hays, Michael Page, and Robert Half have strong Saudi presences and provide access to unadvertised roles.
  • Company career pages: Saudi Aramco, SABIC, STC, Al-Rajhi Bank, and NEOM all post extensively on their own websites — often before jobs reach any aggregator.
  • Networking: Personal introductions remain culturally significant in Saudi Arabia. Attend professional events, join LinkedIn groups focused on your sector, and reach out to Saudi professionals in your field.

4. Understand Saudisation (Nitaqat)

Under the Nitaqat system, private sector companies must maintain a certain percentage of Saudi staff. This works in favour of Saudi nationals — many employers are actively trying to hire qualified Saudi talent to stay compliant. As a Saudi job seeker, mention your nationality clearly and explore the Human Resources Development Fund (Hadaf) portal, which lists thousands of subsidised roles.

If you are an expat, be aware that some sectors have higher Saudisation requirements. Healthcare and IT remain more open to expat hiring than certain government-adjacent or retail roles.

5. Prepare for the Interview Process

Interviews in Saudi Arabia increasingly begin with a video call. Dress conservatively and professionally regardless. Be prepared for questions about your willingness to relocate, your expectations around Ramadan working hours, and your familiarity with local business culture.

Salary negotiation is expected and respected. Research market rates using Glassdoor, LinkedIn Salary, or the Saudi Human Resources Ministry's published data. Remember: in Saudi Arabia, salaries for most roles are tax-free, which significantly increases your effective take-home compared to equivalent roles in Europe or North America.

6. Sort Out Logistics Early

Once you receive an offer, your employer typically sponsors your work visa and iqama (residency permit). However, independently verify the role's legitimacy, confirm the visa type, and read the full employment contract carefully — particularly around notice periods, end-of-service benefits, annual flight allowances, and probation terms — before signing anything.

Pro tip: The fastest successful job seekers in Saudi Arabia apply within 24-48 hours of a listing going live. Set up email alerts on JobOrbit and LinkedIn so you are always first in the queue.

Final Thoughts

Finding a job in Saudi Arabia in 2026 is entirely achievable if you approach it strategically. The market is growing, salaries are competitive, and the country's transformation under Vision 2030 means new industries and roles appear every quarter. Start with a strong CV, use multiple channels in parallel, network actively, and be ready to move quickly when the right opportunity appears.
